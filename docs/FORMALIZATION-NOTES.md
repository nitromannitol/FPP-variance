# Formalization notes

This document lists every place where a Lean statement of this repository differs in form from the printed one, the deviations of Sections 1 and 2, and the places where the formal proofs take a different route from the printed proofs, the notes of Section 4. It covers two papers by Scott Armstrong and Ahmed Bou-Rabee:

- **[L]** *Curvature and variance in lattice first-passage percolation*, the version of October 31, 2026 (18 pages; in this repository, `papers/CurvatureVariance.pdf`), formalized in the library `CurvatureVariance`;
- **[C]** *Fluctuation bounds for rotationally invariant first-passage percolation*, the version submitted to arXiv on October 30, 2026 (19 pages; in this repository, `papers/IsotropicVariance.pdf`), formalized in the library `IsotropicVariance`.

Both papers may still be revised, and every entry refers to these versions. Locations in [L] and [C] are given by page and by the statement, equation and section numbers of these versions, with the LaTeX label in parentheses, for example Lemma 2.5 (`lem:slabs`). [L] cites [C] as [AB26], with its numbering. The cited works are referred to by their own numbering and pages, in the versions the papers cite:

- [AKM] S. Armstrong, T. Kuusi and J.-C. Mourrat, *Quantitative Stochastic Homogenization and Large-Scale Regularity*, Grundlehren der mathematischen Wissenschaften 352, Springer, 2019; both papers refer to the numbering of the book, whose (0.41)–(0.42) are (0.33)–(0.34) of the preliminary version arXiv:1705.05300v2 (in both papers, [AKM19]);
- [BLM] S. Boucheron, G. Lugosi and P. Massart, Concentration inequalities using the entropy method, *Ann. Probab.* 31 (2003), 1583–1614 (in [C], [BLM03]; [L] uses it through the proofs of [C]);

**What is a deviation.** A **deviation** is a deliberate difference between a Lean statement and the printed one: a convention, the reading of an ambiguous phrase, explicit quantifiers and constants in place of "sufficiently large" and $o(1)$, a stronger or a weaker statement that suffices for every use, or a cited result stated in the form the paper uses. How a paper cites a work, and arguments that the formalization carries out in full, are recorded with the deviations of the paper that uses them. Each library states the cited results it uses separately, so these are counted with the paper. Counts are of entries, not of occurrences. Entries are cited by section and number: Deviation L4 is the fourth deviation of Section 1 (the paper [L]), and Deviation C4 the fourth deviation of Section 2 (the paper [C]).

**Effect on the main results.** No entry changes the main results. The four main theorems are proved with no hypothesis beyond the papers' (Deviations L1, C1 and C3 describe how the hypotheses are stated), and they depend only on the axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`:

- Theorem 1.1 of [L] is `CurvatureVariance.lintegral_exp_passageTime_le`;
- Theorem 1.1 of [C] is `IsotropicVariance.lintegral_exp_passageTime_le`;
- Theorem 1.2 of [C] is `IsotropicVariance.lintegral_exp_passageTime_le_of_isWhiteNoise` and `IsotropicVariance.lintegral_exp_passageTime_le_of_isPoissonProcess`.

Theorem 1.1 of [L] and Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 of [C] bound an exponential moment of $T(0,x)$. The lattice library also proves the variance bound stated after Theorem 1.1 of [L] (`CurvatureVariance.Paper.Closing.variance_passageTime_le`), and two variance bounds at the lattice points $\lfloor n\xi\rfloor$ that [L] does not state, with the same three axioms: one uniform in the direction, a corollary of that variance bound (`CurvatureVariance.evariance_passageTime_floorVertex_le_uniform`), and one at a single direction, under a form of (Curv) on an arbitrary transverse hyperplane (`CurvatureVariance.evariance_passageTime_floorVertex_le`). The second is not a corollary of Theorem 1.1: the library proves it from the same statements of Sections 2–7 of [L] (Deviation L2). The continuum library also proves, as corollaries, variance bounds at the points $te_1$ (`IsotropicVariance.variance_passageTime_le`, `IsotropicVariance.variance_passageTime_le_of_isWhiteNoise` and `IsotropicVariance.variance_passageTime_le_of_isPoissonProcess`), and the same bounds at every point (`IsotropicVariance.variance_passageTime_le_norm`, `IsotropicVariance.variance_passageTime_le_norm_of_isWhiteNoise` and `IsotropicVariance.variance_passageTime_le_norm_of_isPoissonProcess`), with the same three axioms, and the variance bound stated after Theorem 1.1 (Deviation C2).

1. **p. 1, Theorem 1.1 (`thm:main`), Assumptions (W) and (Curv), the time constant.** The dimension is $d=m+1$ with $m\geq1$. Vertices are pairs $(s,y)\in\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z^{d-1}$ and points of $\mathbb R^d$ pairs in $\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^{d-1}$ with the Euclidean norm; this product only indexes the coordinates, and no coordinate plays a special role in the statement. Assumption (W) is `IsIIDWeights P F` (the weights are independent with law $F$ under the law $P$ of the configuration) together with `AssumptionW m F` (an exponential moment, and $F(\{0\})\lt p_c(d)$, with $p_c(d)$ given by `bondCriticalProb`); the law $\nu$ of [L] is named $F$, a law on $[0,\infty)$. The time constant enters as a norm $\mu$ (`PtNorm`) with the hypothesis `IsTimeConstant P μ` ($T(0,nx)/n\to\mu(x)$ almost surely for every $x\in\mathbb Z^d$). Its existence under (W) and its uniqueness are proved (`exists_isTimeConstant`, `IsTimeConstant.unique`), as are the existence and uniqueness of the law of the weights (`exists_isIIDWeights`, `IsIIDWeights.unique`), so these hypotheses are satisfiable and determine $P$ and $\mu$. (Curv) with the constants $\delta,r,\Lambda,\lambda$ is `AssumptionCWith μ ξ δ r M c`, in which $\Lambda$ is named `M` and $\lambda$ is named `c`, on the hyperplane $H=\xi^\perp$, which is the subspace `(Submodule.span ℝ {ξ})ᗮ` with the Euclidean norm. The ranges $\delta\in(0,1]$, $r\gt0$, $\Lambda\geq0$ and $\lambda\gt0$ are hypotheses of the theorem rather than part of the definition. The quantifiers are in the order of [L]: first $F$, with $P$ and $\mu$, then the constants of (Curv), then $\varepsilon\gt0$ and $C$, and then every unit vector $\xi$ satisfying (Curv) with these constants and every $x\in\mathbb Z^d\setminus\{0\}$ near the ray. The constant is a real number $C$ with $0\lt C$, where [L] writes $C\lt\infty$, so that the denominator is positive. The phrase "within distance $|x|^{3/4}$ of the ray $\mathbb R_+\xi$" is read as: some $\beta\geq0$ satisfies $|x-\beta\xi|\leq|x|^{3/4}$, which is the same, since the ray is closed. For every such $x$, the conclusion asserts that $T(0,x)$ is almost everywhere measurable, which [L] takes for granted, and bounds the expectation as a lower integral `∫⁻` of `ENNReal.ofReal`, the expectation of a nonnegative variable with values in $[0,\infty]$. Files: [LintegralExpPassageTimeLe.lean](../CurvatureVariance/Statements/Main/LintegralExpPassageTimeLe.lean), [AssumptionCWith.lean](../CurvatureVariance/Statements/Curvature/AssumptionCWith.lean), [Companions](../CurvatureVariance/Statements/Companions).

2. **p. 1, the variance bound after Theorem 1.1, and the bounds at the points $\lfloor n\xi\rfloor$, which [L] does not state.** [L] states: "Theorem 1.1 implies that $\mathrm{Var}[T(0,x)]\leq2C^2|x|^{1-\varepsilon}$ for every such $x$." This is `Paper.Closing.variance_passageTime_le` ([Main.lean](../CurvatureVariance/Paper/Closing/Main.lean)), which adds the variance bound to the conclusion of Theorem 1.1, for the same $\varepsilon$ and $C$. The variance is `evariance`, with values in $[0,\infty]$, which for an almost everywhere measurable variable is finite exactly when the variable is square integrable; so the bound also asserts that $T(0,x)$ is square integrable. The proof derives it from the bound of Theorem 1.1 by $e^t\geq1+t^2/2$. The library also proves two bounds at the lattice points $\lfloor n\xi\rfloor$, the coordinatewise floor (`floorVertex`), which [L] does not state. *Uniform in the direction:* for the constants of (Curv) there are $\varepsilon\gt0$ and $n_0$ such that $\mathrm{Var}[T(0,\lfloor n\xi\rfloor)]\leq n^{1-\varepsilon}$ for every unit vector $\xi$ satisfying (Curv) with these constants and every $n\geq n_0$ (`evariance_passageTime_floorVertex_le_uniform`). It is a corollary of the variance bound after Theorem 1.1, with a smaller $\varepsilon$, since $\lfloor n\xi\rfloor$ lies within distance $|\lfloor n\xi\rfloor|^{3/4}$ of the ray when $n\geq2d$ (`Paper.Closing.floorVertex_near_ray`). *At one direction:* for a vector $\xi\neq0$ and a subspace $H$ with $\mathbb R^d=\mathbb R\xi\oplus H$ (`IsCompl (Submodule.span ℝ {ξ}) H`), under `AssumptionCAt μ ξ H`, there are $\varepsilon\gt0$ and $n_0$, depending on the direction, with the same bound for every $n\geq n_0$ (`evariance_passageTime_floorVertex_le`). `AssumptionCAt μ ξ H` asks for some $\delta\in(0,1]$, $r\gt0$, $\Lambda\geq0$, a linear functional and a positive definite quadratic form on $H$, with the Euclidean norm of $\mathbb R^d$, for which the expansion of (Curv) holds at $\xi$ for $h\in H$ with $|h|\leq r$. This theorem is a result of the formalization alone, not a corollary of Theorem 1.1. Its hypotheses hold, with $H=\xi^\perp$, at every unit vector satisfying (Curv), but they also allow a vector $\xi$ that is not a unit vector and a hyperplane $H$ other than $\xi^\perp$, which Theorem 1.1 does not cover. The library proves it from the same statements of Sections 2–7 and the induction of Section 3, applied to a model built from this one direction (Deviation L10). Files: [EvariancePassageTimeFloorVertexLeUniform.lean](../CurvatureVariance/Statements/Main/EvariancePassageTimeFloorVertexLeUniform.lean), [EvariancePassageTimeFloorVertexLe.lean](../CurvatureVariance/Statements/Main/EvariancePassageTimeFloorVertexLe.lean), [AssumptionCAt.lean](../CurvatureVariance/Statements/Curvature/AssumptionCAt.lean).

4. **p. 6, (2.5) (`eq:classical`), [ADH, Theorems 3.13 and 3.33].** [L] states: "The first bound follows from [ADH17, Theorem 3.13], which gives Gaussian tails for the lower deviations at all scales, and for the upper deviations up to a fixed multiple of $|x|_1$." Both cited theorems are stated under (W). Theorem 3.13 is stated for $x\neq0$ (`measure_abs_passageTime_sub_integral_le`, [MeasureAbsPassageTimeSubIntegralLe.lean](../CurvatureVariance/Statements/Inputs/MeasureAbsPassageTimeSubIntegralLe.lean)), which [ADH] leave implicit: the theorem does not quantify $x$, and at $x=0$, where $T(0,0)=0$, the event of its lower-tail bound is the whole space for every $t\ge0$. [L] uses it only for $x\neq0$. Both of its tails are proved, although [ADH] prove only the upper tail and refer to Damron and Kubota for the lower one. Theorem 3.33 is stated, as in [ADH], for $|x|\gt1$ (`integral_passageTime_le_timeConstant_add`), which for a lattice point is the range $|x|_1\geq2$ in which [L] uses it. (2.5) is `Paper.Contracts.ClassicalStatement` ([Geometry.lean](../CurvatureVariance/Paper/Contracts/Geometry.lean)), which also asserts that $T(0,x)$ is integrable, so that the expectations have a meaning. It is proved along the printed route by `Paper.Proofs.Geometry.classicalStatement_holds` ([Classical.lean](../CurvatureVariance/Paper/Proofs/Geometry/Classical.lean)), with one step that [L] leaves to the reader (Section 4).

5. **p. 6, Section 2, "Kesten's bound on paths of small passage time [Kes86, Proposition 5.8] assumes that $\nu(\{0\})$ is less than the critical probability $p_T(d)$ for the finiteness of the expected cluster size of bond percolation. By the sharpness of the phase transition [DCT16, Theorem 1.1], $p_T(d)=p_c(d)$, so it applies under (W)."** The formalization states Kesten's estimate in the combined form that [L] uses, under (W), as [ADH, proof of Theorem 4.6, p. 77] quotes it: if $F(\{0\})\lt p_c(d)$, there are $a,C_9\gt0$ such that, for all $n\geq1$, the probability that some self-avoiding path from $0$ with at least $n$ edges has passage time less than $an$ is at most $e^{-C_9n}$ (`measure_exists_isPath_walkCost_lt_le`). [Kes86, Proposition 5.8, p. 182] assumes $F(\{0\})\lt p_T(d)$ and bounds this probability by $De^{-En}$; the hypothesis $F(\{0\})\lt p_c(d)$ is the one [L] obtains from $p_T(d)=p_c(d)$, and the prefactor $D$ is absorbed by decreasing the constants. So the formal statement is the result [L] cites, in the form [L] uses; its documentation names [ADH, proof of Theorem 4.6, p. 77], where it is quoted in this form, and Proposition 5.8 of [Kes86] as its origin. The formalization states [DCT, Theorem 1.1(1)], the exponential decay in $n$ of the probability that $0$ is connected to the boundary of the box of radius $n$, for bond percolation with parameter $p\lt p_c(d)$ (`measure_reachable_sphere_le`), and proves Kesten's estimate from it directly under $F(\{0\})\lt p_c(d)$, by a block renormalization that does not use the van den Berg–Kesten inequality; the library has no separate statement of $p_T(d)=p_c(d)$. It also proves $1/(2d)\leq p_c(d)\leq1$ (`bondCriticalProb_bounds`), so that (W) holds for some laws in every dimension. The form printed in [L], an $\mathcal O_1(C)$ bound on the largest $m$ for which some self-avoiding path from $x$ with $m$ edges has passage time at most $cm$, is derived from this estimate inside the proofs, where it gives Lemma 2.3(3) and the bound on the number of edges of geodesics (Deviation L6). [ADH, Theorem 2.5], which [L] cites for the time constant, is Theorem 6.1 of [Kes86] with $F(\{0\})\lt p_c(d)$ in place of Kesten's $F(\{0\})\lt p_T(d)$; the formalization proves it, as $\mu(e_1)\gt0$, from Kesten's estimate (Deviation L3). Nothing is assumed beyond (W). Files: [Statements/Inputs](../CurvatureVariance/Statements/Inputs).

6. **p. 6, Section 2, "Minimizing paths exist [ADH17, Proposition 4.4], and removing their loops gives geodesics", and "every geodesic from $u$ to $v$ has at most $C(1+|v-u|_1)+\mathcal O_1(C)$ edges, for all $u,v\in\mathbb Z^d$".** [ADH, Proposition 4.4, p. 74] is formalized as one statement that combines its two parts into the form [L] uses: if $F(\{0\})\lt p_c(d)$, then almost surely, for all $u,v\in\mathbb Z^d$, some self-avoiding path from $u$ to $v$ has passage time $T(u,v)$ (`ae_exists_isPath_walkCost_eq_passageTime`). The bound on the number of edges is `Paper.Contracts.GeodesicLengthStatement` ([Geometry.lean](../CurvatureVariance/Paper/Contracts/Geometry.lean)): for each pair $u,v$ there is one random variable $X\leq\mathcal O_1(C)$ such that, almost surely, every geodesic and every geodesic in a slab from $u$ to $v$ has at most $C(1+|v-u|_1)+X$ edges. The bound is almost sure, not for every configuration: when all weights vanish, a null event, every self-avoiding path is a geodesic.

10. **p. 4, Section 2, the choice of coordinates, and p. 5, Lemma 2.1 (`lem:chart`); p. 4, the convention on constants; p. 9, the proof of Theorem 1.1.** [L] states: "Since permutations and sign changes of the coordinates preserve the law of the weights, $\mu$ and (Curv), we order and orient the coordinates so that the first component $\xi_1$ of $\xi$ is positive and has the largest absolute value", and "Constants and thresholds depend on $d$, $\nu$ and the constants $\delta,r,\Lambda,\lambda$ of (Curv), but not on $\xi$". Sections 2–7 are formalized for a model in the chosen coordinates (`Paper.Model`): its law (`Paper.Law`: the laws of the weights and of the configuration, the time constant and the exponent $\delta$) and the data of the expansion (2.1) (`eq:curvature`) at a slope $t_0$, namely $p_0$, the quadratic form $h\mapsto h\cdot\Sigma h$ and the constant and radius of the remainder. Its standing hypotheses (`Paper.Model.Standing`) are (W), the time constant, and (2.1) with a positive definite form. 3. **pp. 1–2, Section 1 and Assumption A (`ass:local`): the noises and the density.** Gaussian white noise is a measurable random distribution $W$ on a standard Borel probability space with $W(\varphi)$ centered Gaussian of variance $\int\varphi^2$ for every test function $\varphi$ (`IsWhiteNoise`). Both exist (`exists_isWhiteNoise`, `exists_isPoissonProcess`). 4. **Sections 1.2 and 2–7: independence, locality and resampling of the noise.** The statements of Sections 2–7 are proved over an abstract setting (`Paper.Setting`, `Paper.NoiseModel`, [Model.lean](../IsotropicVariance/Paper/Defs/Model.lean)): a standard Borel probability space with a σ-algebra, "the noise in $S$", for each set $S$, an action of the rigid motions, and a density with the properties of Assumption A. Three of its properties make phrases of [C] precise. [C] states "By the locality in Assumption A, $T_P$ is determined by $\omega|_P$, so the $T^{(i)}$ are independent" (p. 3) and "Since $X_G$ is determined by $\omega|_G$ and distinct blocks have disjoint interiors, the $X_G$ of distinct blocks are independent" (p. 16). The setting provides the independence of the noise in countably many Borel sets with pairwise disjoint interiors and boundaries of measure zero (`NoiseModel.iIndep_info`), which covers the slabs and blocks of [C], and the joint measurability of $\rho(\omega,x)$, over the points $x$ whose closed unit ball lies in an open set $U$, with respect to the noise in $U$ (`Setting.determined_joint`); in the abstract setting, determination at each point separately does not imply it. The arguments that split the noise into pieces (the proofs of Lemma 2.1 and of the second bound of Lemma 6.3) use that the noise in a union is generated, up to null sets, by the noise in the pieces (`NoiseModel.union_ae`), since for white noise the restrictions are defined only up to null sets. The resampling of a block, "the noise $\omega^G$ that coincides with $\omega$ outside $G$ and whose restriction to $G$ is drawn, given $\omega$, from the conditional law of $\omega|_G$ given $\sigma_G$, independently for distinct blocks" (p. 14), is a joint law of the noise and of the resamplings of all blocks, for all choices of the vectors $a_i$ (`Paper.Setting.blockResampling`, [Resampling.lean](../IsotropicVariance/Paper/Defs/Resampling.lean)). In it, $\sigma_G$ is a σ-algebra contained in the noise in $G$, in place of a random variable; $\omega^G$ is drawn from the conditional law of the noise given the noise outside $G$ and $\sigma_G$; and "coincides with $\omega$ outside $G$" means that, almost surely, every event of the noise outside $G$ happens for both or for neither, since the setting has no restriction map from which $\omega^G$ could be assembled. The exchangeability of $(\omega^G,\omega)$ is proved (`NoiseModel.exchangeable_resamplingLaw`). For both noises and every density satisfying Assumption A, there is such a setting whose passage times $T(u,v)$ have the same laws (`Paper.Proofs.Bridge.settingOfRealizationStatement_holds`, [Main.lean](../IsotropicVariance/Paper/Proofs/Bridge/Main.lean)).

5. **p. 7, proof of Lemma 2.1, [BLM, Corollary 3, p. 1585, and Theorem 7, p. 1587]; p. 17, proof of Lemma 6.3, [BLM, Proposition 1, p. 1584].** Corollary 3 is stated as printed (`Cited.blm_corollary3`). The operators $\sqrt c\,(\mathrm{Id}-E_i)$ have both properties, and this instance is proved. Proposition 1.3 is `Paper.Contracts.ModifiedPoincareStatement` ([Cited.lean](../IsotropicVariance/Paper/Contracts/Cited.lean)), proved by `Paper.Proofs.Modified.modifiedPoincareStatement_holds` from this instance with $c=4/\alpha$ and the bound stated after the proposition, "$g^2\le\mathbb E[g^2]/\alpha$ gives $\mathrm{Ent}[g^2]\le\mathrm{Var}[g^2]/\mathbb E[g^2]\le4\mathrm{Var}[g]/\alpha$" (`Infra.Entropy.entropy_sq_le_four_div_mul_variance_of_atoms`). 12. **p. 11, (4.7) (`eq:chainsum`).** `Paper.Contracts.ChainSumStatement` ([Profiles.lean](../IsotropicVariance/Paper/Contracts/Profiles.lean)), proved by `Paper.Proofs.Profiles.chainSumStatement_of`, allows arrays with values in $[0,\infty]$ defined on a larger probability space with a measure-preserving map $\pi$ to the space of the noise, the chain being that of $\pi(\omega)$; independence and the moment bound are under the larger law, and the moment bound is the only integrability hypothesis. ## 4. Differences between the formal proofs and the printed proofs

The formal proofs follow the printed ones, with the following changes of route. Where a note concerns the range or the form of a statement, it says so and names the deviation, if there is one.

**[L].**

- *Kesten's estimate*, which [L] takes from [Kes86, Proposition 5.8] with $p_T(d)=p_c(d)$ by [DCT, Theorem 1.1], is proved directly under $F(\{0\})\lt p_c(d)$ from [DCT, Theorem 1.1(1)], by a block renormalization that avoids the van den Berg–Kesten inequality (Deviation L5).
- *The first bound of (2.5) and Lemma 2.3(1).* [L] takes the upper deviations from [ADH, Theorem 3.13] "up to a fixed multiple of $|x|_1$", and from the Chernoff bound for "the larger upper deviations". The formal proof bounds the deviations between that multiple and the level from which the Chernoff bound applies by the monotonicity of the tail (`Paper.Proofs.Geometry.measure_le_of_gauss_of_chernoff`).
- *Lemma 2.5(1)* takes the aperture $1/(2(1+T))$, with $T$ a bound of $|t_0|$ plus one, in place of $1/(8d^{1/2})$, and joins $u$ and $v$ at the cost $T_P$ to the neighbours of the two vertices $x$ and $x'$ of the proof. *Lemma 2.5(2)* takes the existence of geodesics from Lemma 2.3(3) on the event, rather than from [ADH, Proposition 4.4].
- *The proof of Theorem 1.1* runs the induction for the $2d$ laws of the choice of coordinates, with one $K$ for all of them (Deviation L10).
- *Lemma 6.3* also gives a value of $\omega_L$ for the $L\geq16$ that are not large, which the formal statement needs at every $L\geq16$ (`Paper.Proofs.Residual.exists_coarse_residualBound`).
- *Lemma 6.4.* The number of translates of a block that meet the cells near the segment $[J_{i-1},J_i]$ is bounded by $3+|J_i-J_{i-1}|+2R_i(J_{i-1},J_i)$, which is weaker than the printed $C(1+(|J_i-J_{i-1}|+R_i(J_{i-1},J_i))/L)$ by a factor $L$, and suffices, since (4.4) and (6.2) bound the sum over $i$ of its square by $CK$ in mean (`Paper.Gain.card_translates_meet_tube_le`).
- *Proposition 6.5.* The final comparison is made in $L^2$, by Minkowski's inequality where the proof of [C] uses Jensen's (`Paper.Gain.eLpNorm_averagedMin_sub_le`). If the sum of the $L^1$ terms in the inequality of Benaïm and Rossignol vanishes, the formal proof shows $\mathrm{Var}[f]=0$ by the Efron–Stein inequality, since there the inequality says nothing: in Lean, $\log(V/0)=0$ (`Paper.Gain.Printed.modifiedPoincare_of_labels`).
- *Constants.* Constants and auxiliary choices, such as meshes, cutoffs and thresholds, are made explicitly, sometimes differently from [L], for example $Q_{2^{-j}s}\leq2^{-j/4}Q_s$ in place of $2^{-2j/5}Q_s$ in Lemma 4.1; none changes a statement.

**[C].**

- *Measurability.* [C] treats passage times as random variables without comment. The formal proof shows that $T(u,v)$ is almost everywhere measurable by projection of analytic sets from the standard Borel space of continuous curves of bounded variation (`Infra.Measurability.nullMeasurable_passageTime`, [Passage.lean](../IsotropicVariance/Infra/Measurability/Passage.lean)). Suprema over near-geodesics are handled by the same projection, with jointly measurable versions of the passage times.
- *White noise* is constructed on a standard Borel space as $W(\varphi)=\sum_ja_j(\varphi)\,\omega_j$, with independent standard Gaussians $\omega_j$ and the coefficients $a_j(\varphi)$ of $\varphi$ in local Fourier bases of the unit cubes (`exists_isWhiteNoise`); the Poisson process is constructed from independent point clouds in the unit cubes (`exists_isPoissonProcess`). The setting of Deviation C4 is verified for both.
- *The display after (1.5) (`eq:transverse`), in the overview, p. 4.* Step 2 bounds (1.5) "for two cubes as in Step 1 with $s=n$, and $S$ the slab between their faces", and continues "We also prove that, for every $\eta\gt0$ and some $h\gt0$ depending on $\eta$," followed by the bound $\mathbb E\big[\sup\big\lvert T_S(u,v)-\lvert v-u\rvert-T_S(\tilde u,\tilde v)+\lvert\tilde v-\tilde u\rvert\big\rvert^2\big]^{1/2}\le\eta Q_n$; the cubes of Step 1 (p. 3) have centers that "differ by $se_1$ plus a vector of length at most $s$ orthogonal to $e_1$". Section 5 proves this bound as Lemma 5.2, (5.2) (`eq:modulus`), for the cubes it fixes on p. 11, "cubes $I,I'$ and a slab $S$ as in (4.1) with $s=n$ and $|y|\leq n/2$", and every use of Lemmas 5.1 and 5.2 has $|y|\le n/2$. The library formalizes the display in this range of cubes, the range the proofs use: Lemma 5.2 is `Paper.Contracts.ModulusStatement` ([Localization.lean](../IsotropicVariance/Paper/Contracts/Localization.lean)), for $|y|\le n/2$, with the moment of order $8d$, which bounds the second moment of the display, and the library has no statement of the display for $n/2\lt|y|\le n$. The transverse bound (1.5) is formalized for the cubes of Step 1, with $|y|\le n$ (`Paper.Contracts.OverviewTransverseStatement`, proved by `Paper.Closing.overviewTransverseStatement_holds`, [Overview.lean](../IsotropicVariance/Paper/Closing/Overview.lean)). This restricts the range of an unnumbered display of the overview, whose formal form is Lemma 5.2 as printed; it is recorded here and in Section 5, not as a deviation.
- *Lemma 2.1 (`lem:classical`), p. 7.* The proof reads: "Both tails of the first bound follow from the inequalities of Boucheron, Lugosi and Massart [BLM03, Corollary 3 and Theorem 7], since resampling $\omega$ in one unit cube changes $T_t$ by at most $C$, and the squares of the increases of $T_t$ sum over the cubes to at most $Ct$." The tail bound of [BLM, Theorem 7] is stated for deviations of $Z$ up to $(e-1)\mathbb E[g(Z)]$, where $g$ bounds $V_+$, and here $g$ is a constant of order $t$. The formal proof also uses the deterministic bound $T_t\le(\sup\rho)t$, so that the range of the tail bound contains every deviation: [BLM, Corollary 3 and Theorem 7] (`Cited.blm_corollary3`, `Cited.blm_theorem7`) are applied to $-f/r$ for a function $f$ with $0\le f\le L$, under the hypothesis $L/r\le(e-1)c$, where $c$ bounds $V_+$ (`Paper.Proofs.Classical.isOs_abs_centered_of_neg_proxy`, [BoundedProductConcentration.lean](../IsotropicVariance/Paper/Proofs/Classical/BoundedProductConcentration.lean)), and then no deviation lies beyond that range (`Paper.Proofs.Classical.isOs_abs_centered_of_ae_bounded_positive_proxy`, [ProductConcentrationAE.lean](../IsotropicVariance/Paper/Proofs/Classical/ProductConcentrationAE.lean)). The proof of Lemma 2.1 uses this for a representation $f$ of $T(u,v)$ as a function of the noise in finitely many cubes, with $L=b|v-u|$ for an upper bound $b$ of the density and $r$ a bound of the change of $T(u,v)$ when the noise in one cube is resampled (`Paper.Proofs.Classical.exists_passage_concentration_constant`, [PassageConcentration.lean](../IsotropicVariance/Paper/Proofs/Classical/PassageConcentration.lean)); Theorem 7 is applied to a version with unit differences at every point (Deviation C5). The bound on $\mathbb E[T_t]-t$ for small $t$ and the argument of [HN, Lemma 4.1] are as in Deviation C6; that argument uses a net of mesh $2$ and no exceptional event, since the density is bounded. Lemma 2.1 is `Paper.Contracts.ClassicalBoundsStatement`, proved by `Paper.Proofs.Classical.classicalBoundsStatement_holds`.
- *Lemma 2.2* carries out the argument of [HN, Theorem 2.6 and Lemma 2.7] uniformly in the endpoints, as [C] describes, with a number $W$ that has the properties used in the proof rather than the smallest such number (`Paper.Proofs.Straight.exists_metric_scale`).
- *Lemma 6.2.* The variables $X_G$ are read off versions of $T_G$ that are measurable for the noise in $G$ (`Infra.Slab.exists_setTime_version`), and in Step 3 the values of small probability are merged block by block rather than into one value $\psi^*$; by stationarity, the laws agree.
- *Lemma 6.2 (`lem:labels`), the form of its variables, and the first bound of Lemma 6.3 (`lem:residual`), pp. 15–16.* Lemma 6.2 is formalized with variables $X_G$ that depend only on their block, as its construction gives, which is the form the proof of Lemma 6.3 uses. The printed lemma provides, "For every choice of the vectors $a_i$ and every $i\in\{1,\ldots,K\}$", random variables "$X_G$, one for each block $G$ of the $i$th slab"; the formal statement adds the clause that each $X_G$ depends only on $G$ (`Paper.Contracts.LabelsStatement`, Deviation C13). The proof of the first bound of Lemma 6.3 takes "the $a_i$ independent of the rest and included in probabilities and expectations", and applies (4.7) (`eq:chainsum`), which is stated for "independent random arrays" (p. 11), to arrays $Y_i$ built from the variables of the $i$th slab. In the formal proof, before (4.7) is applied, the arrays $Y_i$ are replaced by their conditional expectations given the noise in the $i$th slab, averaged over the vectors $a_i$. These arrays are measurable for the noise in their slab, hence independent over the slabs, since each $X_G$ depends only on its block, and their moments are at most those of $Y_i$ (`Paper.Proofs.Residual.residualStatement_of`). The event $E$ of the proof uses a cutoff chosen from $\eta$ instead of $L^{1/2}$.
- *Lemma 6.3, second bound.* The conditional Efron–Stein inequality is proved on the coordinates of the noise in the blocks (`Infra.EfronStein.lintegral_sub_condExp_allLabelSigma_le`). Translations do not carry the resampling of a block to that of the translated block, since the conditional laws are chosen block by block, so the step "by stationarity" uses that $\mathbb E[(F^G_m-F_m)^2]$ is the same for every resampling of $G$ given $X_G$ (`Paper.Proofs.Residual.lintegral_labelResampling_eq`).
- *Lemma 6.4.* Adjacent slabs share a face, so the variables of the other blocks are measurable for the noise outside $G$ only up to null sets (`Paper.Setting.LabelFamily.otherSigma_le_aeClosure`), which the proof in [C] uses without comment.
- *Constants.* Constants and auxiliary choices, such as meshes, cutoffs and thresholds, are made explicitly, sometimes differently from [C]; none changes a statement.

## 5. Scope

Every statement of [L] and [C] on which the main theorems depend is formalized, in the forms described in Sections 1–4; the cited results are proved from Mathlib rather than assumed. In [L], every numbered statement and display has a Lean counterpart, and so do the variance bound after Theorem 1.1 (Deviation L2), Lemma 2.5 in its printed form (`Paper.Closing.slabsBounds_holds`), the bound (a) of Section 3 (`Paper.Closing.boundA_holds`) and Proposition 3.1 in its printed form (`Paper.Closing.improvementStatement_holds`), all three in [Main.lean](../CurvatureVariance/Paper/Closing/Main.lean). A few unnumbered items of [L] are not separate Lean statements, and no proof uses them in a form that the library lacks: the lexicographic order of minimizing chains and $J_M=0$ (Deviation L15); the resampling of Lemma 6.1 at a general $\sigma_G$, of which the library constructs the two cases that [L] uses (Deviation L17); the bound (c) of Section 3, a step of the formal proof of Proposition 3.1; the display before (3.3), whose consequence, (3.2) outside the range (3.3), is stated; the bound $|T(0,(\ell',y'))-T(0,(\ell,y))|\leq\mathcal O_1(CK)$ at the start of Section 6, which the formal proof replaces by a bound in $L^2$, enough for its use; and (1.2) for infinitely many variables (Deviation L7).

In [C], every numbered statement and display has a Lean counterpart, and so do the variance bound after Theorem 1.1, the Poisson case of $\psi(\chi*\omega)$ and the bounds of the abstract (Deviations C1 and C2). The display after (1.5) is formalized in the range of cubes of Lemma 5.2, the range the proofs use (Section 4), and the other unnumbered displays of the overview of [C] restate results of its Sections 4–7.

Background citations that the proofs do not use are not formalized: [ADH, Question 13], the results of Basu, Sidoravicius and Sly quoted in the introductions, Kesten's question and the announcement on the limit shape for exponential weights on $\mathbb Z^2$ quoted in [L], and the results quoted in [C] for comparison or as the origin of an idea, among them those of Kesten, Talagrand, Benjamini, Kalai and Schramm, Newman and Piza, Howard and Newman (Theorem 2.4), and Alexander. Checked lists of the errors and gaps found in the two papers of Basu, Sidoravicius and Sly, from written review and not formalized, are in [bss-errata](bss-errata).
